## 5. Fill in `~/mb/work/claude.json`
Edit only text and the call fields; keep its structure.
- `headline`: what changed since the morning (10 to 140 characters).
- `overview`: 2 to 4 sentences on the overnight session and what London has to deal with.
- For each asset: `changed` (what moved and why since the morning report, 10 to 400 characters) and any new `drivers`.
- Session calls also have `today_view` (`kept`, or `flipped` when the session call turns against the morning call) and `flip_trigger`: `level:<price>` (a level price from brief.md that price has broken), `event:<exact calendar title>` or `news:<https link>`. The draft already sets these when the morning invalidation broke.
For each call:
- `bias`: the code's lean (shown in brief.md) or `neutral`. A long or short against a **neutral** lean is allowed only with `news_driven: true`, `confidence: "low"`, and a linked driver for that asset.
- `confidence`: at most the cap in brief.md. Lower it for event risk inside the window (CPI, jobs, Fed, earnings, OPEC, a token unlock), news that cuts the other way, or a low sample. `null` when neutral.
- `invalidation`: for a long or short, one of that asset's level prices exactly as printed in brief.md (support/resistance list or the suggested invalidation), below the reference for a long, above for a short. `null` when neutral.
- `reason`: 10 to 280 characters, plain words: why this bias and which level matters.
Never invent numbers: every figure you write must come from brief.md or a source you cite.
