    What this page is for

    It feeds your own trade plan: a lean for each horizon, the levels that matter, the price that proves the lean wrong, and what is scheduled. It never sets entries or stops, and it does not trade.

    Where the numbers come from

    Prices, indicators, levels and percentages are computed by code on GitHub every 30 minutes, from public exchange and market data. Claude reads the news and writes the words. A checker rejects any report whose numbers differ from the computed ones.

    How "likely" is measured

    Every past day of an asset gets a label: trend (above or below its 50- and 200-day averages), momentum (RSI below 40, 40 to 60, above 60) and volatility (calm, normal or wild for that asset). The page counts how often price was higher after the same horizon on past days with today's label, and shows it next to the asset's normal up-rate on any day.

    The gap between the two is the edge. 56% against a normal 53% is a 3-point edge, not a 56% signal. Week and month windows overlap, so their case counts are divided by the window length. With too few matching days the label gets coarser, down to all days, marked low sample.

    How a call is chosen

    Three technical signals vote for each horizon. Two or three votes the same way, with a measured edge that doesn't point the other way, give the code's lean. Its confidence cap is high only when all three agree, the edge is at least 5 points, there are 60 or more independent cases and the likely range clears the normal rate; medium needs 2 points and 30 cases. Claude may keep the lean or go neutral and may lower the confidence, never raise it or take the other side. A call against a neutral lean has to be news-driven, low confidence and linked.

    How calls are judged

    When a call's window closes, the next run grades it: a long is right if price closed above where it was called, a short if below. Neutral calls are not scored for direction. The scorecard groups results by day, week or month, so that many assets moving together on one day don't count as independent wins, and says plainly whether the calls beat a coin flip yet.
